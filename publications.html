Finite element study of the efficacy of three different loops for bodily protraction of the mandibular molar

Aditi Mehta, Alap Shah, Anisha Prasad, Bharvi Jani, Kinnari Shah, Anirudh Kulkarni, Nattapon Chantarapanich, Samroeng Inglam, Mohammad Fareed, Anand Marya

Scientific Reports 15 (1), 13938

A finite-element model built from a patient CBCT scan compares T, Cherry and open helical loops at 10°, 15° and 20° tip-back for protracting the mandibular second molar. All three loops performed alike; a 15° tip-back moved the molar with negligible tipping and no extrusion or intrusion.

Understanding the role of CFD in microwave-assisted pyrolysis for biomass conversion

A. Raje, A. A. Bhise, D. V. Surya, A. Kulkarni

Journal of Analytical and Applied Pyrolysis 179, 106477

Reviews how CFD helps explain microwave-assisted pyrolysis of biomass, including microwave absorption and the distribution of thermal and electromagnetic fields. It identifies research gaps and argues for pairing simulations with experiments to predict bio-oil, biochar and syngas yields.

Entropy generation in mixed-convection flow of a non-Newtonian Jeffrey fluid through an inclined porous pipe with convective walls under a magnetic field. Entropy generation and Bejan numbers are mapped against the Hartmann, Darcy, Grashof, Brinkman and Biot numbers.

Numerical analysis of the performance improvement of a flat-plate solar collector using conjugated porous blocks

K. Anirudh, S. Dhinakaran

Renewable Energy 172, 382-391

OpenFOAM simulations of a direct-absorption flat-plate solar collector with three conjugated porous blocks along the insulated bottom wall. The blocks push flow toward the absorber plate; performance is best with a short block at the inlet and a tall one at the outlet.

Numerical investigation of unsteady flow across tandem square cylinders near a moving wall at Re= 100

R. S. Rajpoot, K. Anirudh, S. Dhinakaran

Case Studies in Thermal Engineering 26, 101042

Flow past two tandem square cylinders near a moving wall at Re = 100, for spacing ratios 0.5–8 and wall-gap ratios 0.1–4. The moving wall largely decides when vortex shedding starts and stops; drag is highest on a lone cylinder and lowest on the downstream one.

On the effects of orientation on flow and heat transfer from a semi-circular cylinder near a stationary wall

R. S. Rajpoot, K. Anirudh, S. Dhinakaran

Case Studies in Thermal Engineering 26, 100967

Forced convection from a heated semi-circular cylinder in the shear layer of a stationary wall, with its curved or flat face upstream, at Re = 100–200 and gap ratios 0.1–0.5. The curved-forebody orientation gives the largest heat-transfer enhancement; Nusselt number correlations are provided.

K. Anirudh, S. Dhinakaran

OpenFOAM study of a flat-plate solar collector channel filled with porous metal foam, modelling radiation with the Rosseland approximation and buoyancy at inclinations up to 45°. The foam improves thermal mixing, and the largest gains come at intermediate tilt angles.

Lattice Boltzmann simulations of flow and heat transfer from a permeable triangular cylinder under the influence of aiding buoyancy

T. R. Vijaybabu, K. Anirudh, S. Dhinakaran

International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer 117, 799-817

Lattice Boltzmann simulations of a permeable triangular cylinder, apex-facing and side-facing, under forced convection and aiding buoyancy for Re = 1–40 and Da = 10⁻⁶–10⁻². LBM simulation of unsteady flow and heat transfer from a diamond-shaped porous cylinder

T. R. Vijaybabu, K. Anirudh, S. Dhinakaran

International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer 120, 267-283

Lattice Boltzmann simulations of unsteady flow and heat transfer around a permeable diamond-shaped cylinder for Re = 50–150 and Da = 10⁻⁶–10⁻². Higher permeability weakens vortex shedding, lowers drag and raises heat transfer; Nusselt number correlations and a comparison with a square cylinder are given.

On the onset of vortex shedding past a two-dimensional porous square cylinder

K. Anirudh, S. Dhinakaran

Journal of Wind Engineering and Industrial Aerodynamics 179, 200-214

Locates the onset of vortex shedding behind a porous square cylinder for 41 ≤ Re ≤ 50 across a range of permeability and porosity, using OpenFOAM with the Darcy–Brinkman–Forchheimer model. A shedding-frequency correlation is given, with unsteady results up to Re = 150.

Mixed convective heat transfer from a permeable square cylinder: A lattice Boltzmann analysis

T. R. Vijaybabu, K. Anirudh, S. Dhinakaran

International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer 115, 854-870

Lattice Boltzmann analysis of mixed convection from a porous square cylinder with aiding buoyancy, for Re = 2–40, Da = 10⁻⁶–10⁻² and Ri = 0–1. At Re = 40 and Da = 10⁻², buoyancy raises the mean Nusselt number by 18% at Ri = 0.5 and 34% at Ri = 1 over forced convection.
