Simulating flow, heat, and forces, from stepwells to braces.

RudhLab is a computational research group at Pandit Deendayal Energy University (PDEU), Gandhinagar, led by Anirudh Kulkarni. We write our own simulation software and run it on graphics processors (GPUs) to study how air, water, and heat move, and how teeth respond to orthodontic forces. The problems differ; the toolkit is the same.

Research

Three threads share one toolkit: physics-based models solved with software we write and run on GPUs.

Fluid dynamics and heat transfer

How air and water flow around objects and carry heat: wakes behind cylinders and spheres, flow through porous materials, vibrating structures, and droplets. Studied with finite-volume, lattice-Boltzmann, and immersed-boundary solvers.

Simulations →

Dental mechanics

How teeth and orthodontic appliances respond to the forces of treatment, using finite-element models, including models built from patient CBCT scans: molar protraction loops and implant-supported systems.

Publications →

GPU solvers and research code

Simulation software we write ourselves in CUDA C++ and Julia and run on graphics processors: interface capturing, pressure solvers, and fluid–structure interaction. Public code is on GitHub.

Finite element study of the efficacy of three different loops for bodily protraction of the mandibular molar

Aditi Mehta, Alap Shah, Anisha Prasad, Bharvi Jani, Kinnari Shah, Anirudh Kulkarni, Nattapon Chantarapanich, Samroeng Inglam, Mohammad Fareed, Anand Marya

Scientific Reports 15 (1), 13938 · 2025

A finite-element model built from a patient CBCT scan compares T, Cherry and open helical loops at 10°, 15° and 20° tip-back for protracting the mandibular second molar. All three loops performed alike; a 15° tip-back moved the molar with negligible tipping and no extrusion or intrusion.

Understanding the role of CFD in microwave-assisted pyrolysis for biomass conversion

A. Raje, A. A. Bhise, D. V. Surya, A. Kulkarni

Journal of Analytical and Applied Pyrolysis 179, 106477 · 2024

Reviews how CFD helps explain microwave-assisted pyrolysis of biomass, including microwave absorption and the distribution of thermal and electromagnetic fields. It identifies research gaps and argues for pairing simulations with experiments to predict bio-oil, biochar and syngas yields.

Advances in computational fluid dynamics modeling for biomass pyrolysis: a review

A. Kulkarni, G. Mishra, S. Palla, P. Ramesh, D. V. Surya, T. Basak

Energies 16 (23), 7839 · 2023

A review of CFD modelling for biomass pyrolysis in conventional and microwave-assisted reactors: reaction kinetics and governing equations, the role of microwave wavelength and penetration depth, and the main modelling challenges, with directions for future work.
